Two magnet sensors, one per lane, wired to an ESP32. Drawn for the common 30-pin ESP32 DevKit with USB-C and the KY-035 sensor module. We'll check every pin against the real parts when they arrive.

Wire by wire

Seven jumper wires. Jumper packs come in mixed colours; use any, but keep one colour per job so it's easy to check. Label each sensor's wires with a strip of tape.

Under the track

Looking along the track at the timing line, cut through a plain straight piece. Each kart's magnet rides over the solid strip in the middle of its lane: the slot and the two steel rails.

Outer pocketPut each sensor in the pocket on the outside of its lane's centre strip. It's as close to its own kart as the inside pocket, and further from the other lane.
Face the stripBend the legs so the bead stands on its edge with the printed side (49E) facing the strip. Beside a magnet the field points sideways. Also try it lying flat; keep whichever the live readout shows bigger.
Find the spotRoll a kart over by hand and slide the sensor along the strip until the reading peaks, then fix it with Blu-Tack.
Cover the legsThe steel rails carry power to the cars. Wrap the sensor legs and pins in electrical tape so nothing bare can touch the rails or their tabs underneath.
Wires out the sideRun each sensor's three wires along the underside and out under the nearest edge. The far lane's wires go round the end of the piece to the board.

Before plugging in the USB cable

  1. Every + goes to 3V3. Never use VIN or 5V: more than 3.3 V on an input pin can damage the ESP32.
  2. Lane 1's S goes to D34 and lane 2's to D35. These keep working with Wi-Fi on; many other analogue pins don't.
  3. Check the labels printed on your boards, not just this drawing. On a 38-pin ESP32 the same names sit in different places, and sensor pin order varies between sellers.
  4. Nothing bare near the track's rails: legs and pins taped.
